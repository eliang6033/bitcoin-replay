    How the money works

    Market chance comes from the room’s prices. Player counts show how many classmates hold each side. If you hold both, you count once on each side. NPCs trade separately.

    Up wins if Bitcoin ends above its starting price. Down wins if it ends at or below the start. Each winning share pays $10,000 in practice cash. A losing share pays $0. Sell shares you own before the finish to take the cash shown.

    Choose Up or Down, select Buy or Sell, then choose a percentage and press the order button. The percentage applies to your available cash when buying and the shares you hold when selling. “Cash out everything” sells both sides in one step.

    You can spend only the practice cash in your account. The most you can lose on a bet is what you paid.

    Prices move as the class trades. A bet can cost up to $150 more per share than the estimate, within your spending limit. Cashing out can return up to $150 less per share. A larger change asks you to check the new price.

    The same four hours on the blockchain

    Bitcoin is digital money. Miners compete to add blocks of transactions to its public record, and other computers check those blocks. Watch each block appear at its recorded time.

    What do these counts tell us?

    Each block contributes its recorded transaction count, including its miner reward transaction. Your classroom trades use practice dollars. The electricity used by miners depends on equipment and operating conditions, and the emissions depend on the power supply.

    Class discussion

    Who gets a say when a mine runs overnight?

    In Granbury, Texas, Cheryl Shadden described hearing a Bitcoin mine day and night. In 2024, Earthjustice represented residents seeking a court order to limit the noise and vibration. Their experience raises a question our price chart leaves out.

    At your table

    Imagine a mine offers compensation to run through the night. What condition would you ask for first? Who could enforce it?

    Across the room

    Trade proposals with another table. Which benefits go to miners? Who bears the noise, and what would you need to learn about local electricity and emissions?
