How tickets work

Compare Bitcoin’s final price to its starting price. Higher wins if it finishes above the start. Lower wins if it finishes below or exactly at the start. A winning ticket pays 10 points. Other tickets pay zero.

You can sell tickets you own before time runs out. Everyone in the room trades at the same market. The two buttons show the estimated total for your chosen number of tickets.

A trade can fill up to 0.15 points per ticket above the displayed buy price or below the displayed sell price. If the price changes more than that, you can try again at the new price.

What do these counts tell us?

Each block contributes its recorded transaction count, including its miner reward transaction. Your classroom trades use virtual points. The electricity used by miners depends on equipment and operating conditions, and the emissions depend on the power supply.

Class discussion

Who gets a say when a mine runs overnight?

In Granbury, Texas, Cheryl Shadden described hearing a Bitcoin mine day and night. In 2024, Earthjustice represented residents seeking a court order to limit the noise and vibration. Their experience raises a question our price chart leaves out.

At your table

Imagine a mine offers compensation to run through the night. What condition would you ask for first? Who could enforce it?

Across the room

Trade proposals with another table. Which benefits go to miners? Who bears the noise, and what would you need to learn about local electricity and emissions?
