    How the money works

    Market chance comes from the room’s prices. Player counts show how many classmates hold each side. If you hold both, you count once on each side. NPCs trade separately.

    Up wins if Bitcoin ends above its starting price. Down wins if it ends at or below the start. Dollar purchases can include part of a share. Each full winning share pays $10,000 in practice cash, and fractions pay proportionally. A losing share pays $0. You can sell a bet before the finish.

    Pick a dollar amount, then press Buy Up or Buy Down. This places your bet immediately. Under Your open bets, Sell half and Sell all return money to your available cash.

    Total value includes your available cash and what you would receive by selling all open bets now. Profit or loss is that total minus your starting $100,000. Prices can move before you sell. Buying transfers cash into a bet, so spending $25,000 does not by itself mean a $25,000 loss.

    All cash spends your full available balance. You can spend only money you have. Combined cash-out accounts for how selling both sides changes the price, so it can differ from adding the separate estimates.

    Dollar purchases spend the displayed amount. If the bet available for that amount drops by more than 3% before execution, you will be asked to try again. Sale estimates can move by up to 3% before execution.

    What do these counts tell us?

    Each block contributes its recorded transaction count, including its miner reward transaction. Your classroom trades use practice dollars. The electricity used by miners depends on equipment and operating conditions, and the emissions depend on the power supply.

    Class discussion

    Who gets a say when a mine runs overnight?

    In Granbury, Texas, Cheryl Shadden described hearing a Bitcoin mine day and night. In 2024, Earthjustice represented residents seeking a court order to limit the noise and vibration. Their experience raises a question our price chart leaves out.

    At your table

    Imagine a mine offers compensation to run through the night. What condition would you ask for first? Who could enforce it?

    Across the room

    Trade proposals with another table. Which benefits go to miners? Who bears the noise, and what would you need to learn about local electricity and emissions?
